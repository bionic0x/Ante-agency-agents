# 🧭 Runbook: Strategic Decision & Transition

> **Mode**: NEXUS-Sprint | **Duration**: Decision-dependent | **Agents**: 6-10

---

## Scenario

Use this runbook when the problem is not merely “how do we execute this task?” but **what should we do, why should it work despite other actors, what must receive priority, and how will we know when to adapt or stop?**

Typical cases:

- major resource allocation or reallocation;
- market/category/portfolio choice;
- organizational transformation;
- high-dependency partnership or negotiation;
- response to a competitor, regulator, supplier, platform, or coalition;
- strategic program with meaningful irreversibility or externality;
- an initiative that has succeeded tactically but may have reached culmination;
- transition from extraordinary intervention to ordinary institutional ownership.

Do **not** use this runbook to add ceremony to a low-materiality tactical task. The method scales to the cost of being wrong.

## Governing Doctrine

Read `strategy/GENERAL-STRATEGY-DOCTRINE.md` before the first material recommendation.

Core rule:

```text
purpose
→ geometry
→ causal model
→ decisive dependency / point of intervention
→ options
→ reaction
→ concentration + reserve
→ evidence + adaptation
→ transition + conservation
```

The human decision owner retains final authority. Agents can diagnose, challenge, recommend, execute bounded work, and surface evidence; they do not acquire political/enterprise authority because they can act.

## Agent Roster

### Strategic Core

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| General Strategy Director | Owns framing, causal architecture, eight phases, handoffs, and decision record |
| Research Synthesist | Builds source-grounded evidence base and competing explanations |
| Business Strategist | Develops business/competitive options where commercial structure is material |
| Strategic Red Team | Independently challenges indispensable assumptions before commitment |

### Decision Support

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| Master Plan Architect | Converts the chosen logic into an implementation architecture and attacks hidden dependencies |
| Chief Financial Officer | Tests resource conversion, economics, affordability, reserve, and tail obligations |
| Organizational Psychologist | Maps internal incentives, authority, adoption, and coalition dynamics |

### Governance & Execution Support

| Agent | Role |
|---|---|
| Legal Compliance Checker | Identifies hard legal/regulatory limits; admissibility is not a tradeable score |
| Project Shepherd | Sequences execution and handoffs after strategic choice |
| Reality Checker | Verifies claims of readiness/result against actual evidence |

## Phase 1 — Define the End State

**Lead**: General Strategy Director

Produce:

- purpose;
- desired result;
- minimum acceptable/sufficient result;
- beneficiaries and affected parties;
- horizon;
- unacceptable deterioration;
- hard ethical/legal/institutional limits;
- termination condition.

Do not substitute a KPI, deliverable, launch, signature, or technical milestone for the superior result.

### Gate 1

**Would the decision owner still regard this as a valuable result after accounting for the proposed method and foreseeable side effects?**

---

## Phase 2 — Map Actors, Authority, and Dependency

**Lead**: General Strategy Director  
**Support**: Research Synthesist, Organizational Psychologist

Map:

- decision makers;
- executors;
- resource owners;
- vetoes/blockers;
- formal vs effective authority;
- preferences vs mandates;
- capacity to commit;
- internal audiences;
- relevant third parties;
- dependencies, substitutes, and substitution time;
- who benefits and who bears costs.

### Gate 2

**Can the actors the strategy depends on actually commit, execute, verify, or sustain what the plan assumes?**

---

## Phase 3 — Build the Causal Model

**Lead**: General Strategy Director  
**Support**: Research Synthesist

Write:

```text
If we do X,
we expect Y,
because Z changes,
provided C remains true.
```

Separate immediate output from strategic effect. Add:

- at least one alternative explanation;
- critical assumptions;
- expected observations;
- refutators;
- counterfactual/current-course baseline.

### Evidence categories

Keep separate:

- established facts for the analysis;
- hypotheses;
- attributed intentions;
- unknowns.

### Gate 3

**What observation would make us stop believing the preferred mechanism?**

---

## Phase 4 — Identify Control, Dependencies, and Schwerpunkt

**Lead**: General Strategy Director  
**Support**: Business Strategist, CFO where relevant

Distinguish:

- Centre of Gravity hypothesis — central source of capability/cohesion;
- Schwerpunkt — focus of our own effort;
- decisive point — condition opening the next meaningful possibility;
- critical vulnerability — essential, feasible-to-alter requirement.

Do not force a Centre of Gravity label if direct capability/dependency analysis is clearer.

Define:

- what condition must be controlled or guaranteed;
- what receives concentration;
- minimum sufficient support elsewhere;
- deliberate weakness accepted;
- reserve.

### Gate 4

**Are we acting on what is decisive or merely on what is visible, familiar, measurable, or easy to reach?**

---

## Phase 5 — Design Real Options

**Lead**: Business Strategist / General Strategy Director

Options must differ in **mechanism**, not merely intensity.

Include:

- current course / do nothing, with its real cost;
- preferred option;
- at least one materially different route;
- staged/reversible route when useful;
- transition/exit implications.

Compare:

- contribution to sufficient result;
- assumptions;
- resources;
- time;
- reversibility;
- dependency;
- legitimacy/admissibility;
- sustainment burden.

### Gate 5

**Can another option achieve the minimum acceptable result with fewer fragile assumptions or lower irreversible exposure?**

---

## Phase 6 — Submit Options to Reaction

**Lead**: Strategic Red Team

Model:

- competent opposition;
- substitution/adaptation;
- cooperation that changes the need for the plan;
- third-party response;
- internal coalition/incentive response;
- escalation;
- second-order effects on legitimacy and future options.

The Red Team runs the seven coherence tests:

1. purpose;
2. causal;
3. interactive;
4. conversion;
5. legitimacy;
6. epistemic;
7. exit.

Findings are classified as:

- fatal defect;
- accepted risk;
- pending information.

### Gate 6

**Does the strategy remain coherent after a competent second move?**

## Phase 7 — Execute with Intent, Reserve, and Review

**Lead**: Project Shepherd / relevant domain specialists  
**Governance**: General Strategy Director

Issue bounded handoffs containing:

- superior purpose;
- required result;
- causal role of the specialist;
- hard constraints;
- propose/decide/execute/pause/stop rights;
- information and evidence duties;
- escalation conditions;
- review triggers.

Each trigger maps to a decision:

```text
continue | adjust | pause | escalate | stop
```

Do not use dashboards that have no decision relationship.

### Gate 7

**Can we detect that the mechanism is failing before all resources/options are committed?**

---

## Phase 8 — Transition and Conserve

**Lead**: General Strategy Director  
**Support**: Project Shepherd, CFO, Reality Checker, relevant institutional owner

Distinguish:

1. cessation;
2. closure decision/agreement;
3. transition executed;
4. conservation under ordinary conditions.

Before declaring completion, verify:

- what has actually changed;
- open obligations;
- transition exposure;
- permanent owner;
- ordinary budget/capacity;
- verification procedure;
- exception and revision path;
- retained knowledge/capability;
- conditions for restart or re-diagnosis.

### Gate 8

**What remains functioning when extraordinary attention and resources are removed?**

---

## Strategic Pathology Check

Before final recommendation and before major scale-up, scan for:

- level inversion;
- nominal objective;
- imaginary Centre of Gravity;
- concept confusion;
- dispersion;
- frictionless plan;
- disoriented speed;
- denied culmination;
- reflex symmetry;
- illegitimate victory;
- intention attribution;
- metric substitution;
- escalation of commitment;
- chronic personalization instead of institutionalization.

## Autonomy Rule

Automated systems remain subordinate to purpose, decision rights, evidence, and termination.

Claims about autonomous escalation, saturation, compressed authorization, or similar frontier phenomena are **structured conjectures** unless independently supported in the case. They may justify a test or reserve; they may not be used as self-validating doctrine.

## Completion Standard

The runbook is complete only when the decision owner can answer:

1. What result is sufficient?
2. Who can make or block it?
3. Why should the chosen action cause it?
4. What are we concentrating, and what receives less?
5. How can relevant actors react?
6. What is fact, what is hypothesis, what is attributed intention, and what remains unknown?
7. What evidence would make us change course?
8. How do we stop, transfer, and conserve the result?
