# General Strategy Doctrine

> **Status**: Cross-agency operating doctrine derived from the *Marco Teórico General de la Estrategia* (developed edition, 10 September 2026). It governs strategic work across divisions. It is not a substitute for domain expertise, evidence, law, ethics, or the human decision owner.

## 1. Constitutional thesis

Strategy is the disciplined conversion of limited resources into a valuable result under uncertainty and interaction, when other actors may oppose, adapt, cooperate, delay, reinterpret, or change the conditions of action.

A technically excellent action can still be strategically wrong. Therefore every agent must preserve the hierarchy:

```text
purpose / superior outcome
→ strategic explanation
→ operational sequence
→ tactical objective
→ technical execution
```

Information may travel upward as evidence. Authority and intent travel downward as decisions. Lower-level success never silently rewrites the superior purpose.

## 2. Epistemic status

Keep four statuses distinct:

1. **Inherited doctrine** — traceable to an identifiable primary source or doctrinal tradition.
2. **Contemporary academic construct** — reconstruction, critique, or model from scholarship/social science.
3. **Framework synthesis** — operational definitions, connections, and judgment rules adopted here.
4. **Structured conjecture** — prospective proposition with mechanism, conditions, expected observations, and refutators, but insufficient evidence for doctrinal use.

The fourth category is quarantined. Prospective claims may generate tests or questions; they must not silently become stable rules.

### Transfer rule

Cross-domain analogy is not proof. When importing a concept from military strategy, negotiation, intelligence, institutional theory, or another field, state what relation is preserved and what does not transfer.

### Heuristic rule

Concentration, decentralization, reserve, speed, commitment, and similar recommendations are conditional tensions, not universal commands.

## 3. Levels and primacy

For any material engagement, identify:

| Level | Governing question |
|---|---|
| Purpose / political | What condition is valuable, for whom, within what limits? |
| Strategic | How can resources and forms of action create that condition despite reactions? |
| Operational | How must related actions be sequenced, sustained, and transitioned? |
| Tactical | What immediate interaction must be resolved? |
| Technical | How should the instrument perform reliably? |

### Anti-inversion rule

Ask:

> Can this local metric, task, or technical result improve while the superior outcome worsens?

If yes, it cannot govern the decision by itself.

Common inversion mechanisms:

- metric substitutes for purpose;
- available tool defines the problem;
- prior commitment is defended because it is costly or visible;
- local units optimize their own result while externalizing cost to the whole.

## 4. End state, sufficient success, and limits

Before planning, define:

- desired result;
- minimum acceptable result;
- beneficiaries and affected parties;
- horizon;
- non-negotiable ethical, legal, financial, or institutional limits;
- conditions that make additional gains worth additional effort;
- conditions that justify stopping, adapting, or revising the purpose.

The maximum attainable result is not automatically the preferred result.

Past cost does not justify future cost. It matters only through present assets, obligations, dependencies, damage, and constraints.

## 5. Geometry: actors, authority, power, and dependency

Do not treat an organization, market, government, client, or opposing party as a unitary actor unless that simplification explains the relevant decision.

Map:

- preferences;
- representation/mandate;
- capacity to commit;
- authority to decide;
- ability to execute;
- resource control;
- veto/blocking power;
- internal audiences;
- third parties;
- formal vs effective decision paths;
- substitution options and switching time;
- who receives benefit and who bears cost.

Power is relational. Resources matter through conversion into effects in a specific relationship.

### Dependency test

For each material dependency:

```text
resource/function controlled
× importance to required capability
× scarcity of substitutes
× time to substitute
× duration effect must persist
```

Do not collapse this into a universal score.

## 6. Sequential and cumulative action

Distinguish:

- **Sequential logic** — one achieved condition enables the next action.
- **Cumulative logic** — multiple partially independent actions contribute to an aggregate effect.

For sequential paths, verify that a completed activity actually created the condition needed for the next step.

For cumulative paths, verify that effects persist rather than being absorbed, replaced, offset, or decayed before they matter.

## 7. Centre of Gravity, Schwerpunkt, decisive point, critical vulnerability

Never use these as synonyms.

- **Centre of Gravity** — diagnostic hypothesis about a central source of capability or cohesion whose alteration would propagate materially through the delimited system.
- **Schwerpunkt** — the chosen focus/concentration of our own effort.
- **Decisive Point** — a condition, function, place, or event that opens a relevant advantage or next step.
- **Critical Vulnerability** — an essential requirement that can feasibly be altered with significant effect before the system compensates.

A named actor, technology, location, or asset is not a Centre of Gravity merely because it is visible or important.

Use the causal chain:

```text
dependency
→ capability/cohesion
→ critical requirements
→ feasible vulnerability or point of intervention
→ expected system effect
→ contribution to superior purpose
```

If the Centre of Gravity concept does not improve the explanation, do not force it. Use direct capability/dependency analysis instead.

## 8. Concentration, economy, and reserve

Strategy requires unequal allocation under scarcity.

- **Concentration** — enough resource at the chosen Schwerpunkt to produce a material effect.
- **Economy** — minimum sufficient support elsewhere, accepting deliberate secondary weakness where necessary.
- **Reserve** — deliberately uncommitted material, temporal, political, financial, informational, or decisional capacity.

A priority without a corresponding sacrifice is not yet a priority.

The marginal question is:

> Where does the next unit of resource create the greatest strategic leverage against the current decisive dependency or constraint?

Historical average performance does not decide the next allocation by itself.

## 9. Friction, fog, orientation, and command

Assume friction and uncertainty persist:

- errors;
- delays;
- coordination loss;
- fatigue;
- ambiguous data;
- false precision;
- noisy dashboards;
- authorization latency;
- changing interpretation.

Separate four times:

1. detection time;
2. interpretation time;
3. authorization time;
4. effect time.

Speed in one stage does not guarantee strategic speed.

### Intent-based execution

Decentralize method when local information is superior and errors are bounded. Centralize or escalate when decisions create irreversible, cross-system, legal, ethical, or shared-asset consequences.

Every delegated agent should understand:

- purpose;
- desired result;
- hard constraints;
- decisions it owns;
- information it must report;
- conditions requiring escalation.

Centralize criteria where necessary, decentralize application where useful, centralize high-impact exceptions.

## 10. Interaction, reaction, and paradox

Treat every plan as a hypothesis exposed to other actors.

Before commitment, model at least:

- competent opposition;
- plausible adaptation;
- cooperation that changes the need for the original plan;
- third-party response;
- internal political/organizational response;
- second-order effects on legitimacy, incentives, and future options.

Do not design only against a passive environment.

A locally effective action may worsen the strategic result through reaction, escalation, imitation, substitution, loss of cooperation, or changing expectations.

## 11. Legitimacy, effectiveness, and admissibility

Keep separate:

- **effectiveness** — does it produce the intended effect?;
- **perceived legitimacy** — will relevant actors accept the authority/process enough to sustain the result?;
- **admissibility** — is the action ethically and legally permitted within applicable constraints?

Admissibility is a boundary, not a score that strategic upside can compensate.

Evaluate tactical success by what it does to the cooperation, authority, trust, and institutional capacity required to consolidate the superior result.

## 12. Epistemic discipline and strategic intelligence

Every analytical product must preserve four categories:

| Category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Established fact for the analysis | Source, date, quality, scope, revision path |
| Hypothesis | Alternatives, assumptions, refutators |
| Attributed intention | Cautious language; separate capability from willingness |
| Unknown | Keep visible; decide whether information is worth acquiring |

Do not allow synthesis to erase uncertainty labels.

### Intelligence functions

Strategic intelligence should:

1. detect relevant change;
2. interpret meaning;
3. estimate plausible developments;
4. make consequences for decisions explicit.

It does not own the value judgment or final decision.

### Probability, confidence, impact

Track separately:

- probability — expected likelihood;
- confidence — strength of evidence/reasoning;
- impact — consequence if true.

Low probability, high impact may justify reserve or reversible preparation. High probability with low confidence may justify further evidence before closing options.

### Institutional challenge

A red team is real only if it has:

- relevant competence;
- evidence access;
- incentive independence;
- a route to the decision owner;
- capacity to trigger a pause before irreversible commitment.

A challenge performed after public commitment is often legitimization theatre.

## 13. Eight-phase method

The phases are logical functions, not waterfall bureaucracy. Evidence may force return to an earlier phase.

### Phase 1 — Define the end state

Produce:

- purpose;
- sufficient/minimum result;
- horizon;
- beneficiaries;
- limits;
- termination criteria.

Review question: **Would we still call this success if achieved by the proposed course?**

### Phase 2 — Map actors and geometry

Produce:

- authority map;
- capability map;
- dependencies;
- formal/effective control;
- substitution time;
- relevant third parties.

Review question: **Can the actor we are relying on actually commit and execute what is promised?**

### Phase 3 — Build the causal model

Use:

```text
If we do X,
we expect Y,
because Z changes,
provided conditions C hold.
```

Also write at least one alternative explanation and observations that would reduce confidence.

Review question: **What would make us stop believing this action can produce the end?**

### Phase 4 — Identify control, centres, and decisive points

Define required control/guarantee, decisive dependencies, point of intervention, Schwerpunkt, minimum support elsewhere, and reserve.

Review question: **Are we acting on what is decisive or merely what is accessible and visible?**

### Phase 5 — Design real options

Options must differ in mechanism, not only intensity. Include current-course costs and, when useful, staged/reversible paths.

Review question: **Can another option secure the minimum acceptable result with less dependence on uncertain assumptions?**

### Phase 6 — Submit options to reaction

Model competent response, adaptation, cooperation, third-party shifts, escalation, and second moves.

Review question: **Does the plan require others to remain passive or interpret us exactly as intended?**

### Phase 7 — Execute with reserve and review criteria

Define intent, rights, limits, sequence, observations, thresholds, escalation, reserve, and response to each threshold.

Every indicator must have a decision relationship: continue, adjust, pause, escalate, or stop.

Review question: **Can we discover that the mechanism is failing before committing everything?**

### Phase 8 — Execute transition and conserve the result

Define:

- closure decision;
- outstanding obligations;
- transition sequence;
- permanent owner;
- ordinary resources;
- verification;
- maintenance;
- review/restart conditions.

Review question: **What will still function after extraordinary attention is withdrawn?**

## 14. Seven coherence tests

These are contradiction tests, not a weighted score.

1. **Political/purpose** — Does the immediate result serve the superior purpose?
2. **Causal** — Is there an explicit explanation of how action creates effect?
3. **Interactive** — Does the design survive competent reaction/adaptation?
4. **Conversion** — Do available resources actually convert into control or decision?
5. **Legitimacy** — Does the method preserve the cooperation/authority needed to exploit success?
6. **Epistemic** — Are facts, hypotheses, attributed intentions, and unknowns separated?
7. **Exit** — Are criteria defined to adapt, transfer, stop, terminate, and conserve?

Classify findings as:

- fatal defect;
- accepted risk;
- pending information.

One fatal contradiction can invalidate the plan regardless of how many other tests pass.

## 15. Fourteen strategic pathologies

Scan explicitly for:

1. **Level inversion** — tactical metric replaces superior result.
2. **Nominal objective** — end stated without observable success condition.
3. **Imaginary Centre of Gravity** — visible target mistaken for systemic dependency.
4. **Concept confusion** — CoG, Schwerpunkt, decisive point, vulnerability conflated.
5. **Dispersion** — every priority funded, none decisive.
6. **Frictionless plan** — success requires perfect coordination/data/timing.
7. **Disoriented speed** — faster action on a false model.
8. **Denied culmination** — early success justifies continuation after marginal sign changes.
9. **Reflex symmetry** — competing where the other actor converts resources better.
10. **Illegitimate victory** — method destroys authority/cooperation required to sustain result.
11. **Intent attribution** — psychological inference treated as fact.
12. **Metric substitution** — indicator optimized at expense of the end.
13. **Escalation of commitment** — past cost used to justify more future cost.
14. **Chronic personalization** — recurring conflict requires renegotiation from zero because no institution absorbs it.

## 16. Institutionalization

When conflict or coordination recurs, convert successful ad hoc arrangements into minimum viable institutional architecture:

- rule;
- authority;
- verification;
- exception process;
- appeal/escalation path;
- revision mechanism;
- knowledge retention;
- ordinary resource owner.

Institutionalization should reduce arbitrary renegotiation without fossilizing rules after their conditions disappear.

## 17. Termination and conservation

Distinguish four states:

1. **Cessation** — immediate confrontational activity stops.
2. **Closure decision/agreement** — conditions are established.
3. **Transition executed** — transfers, guarantees, and initial obligations occur.
4. **Conservation** — result survives under ordinary conditions and plausible disturbance.

A strategy matures when the valuable result can persist without continuously reproducing the extraordinary effort that obtained it.

Reserve closing capacity from the beginning. Do not consume all resources winning an agreement that cannot be implemented or maintained.

## 18. Sixteen governing principles

1. Superior purpose governs lower levels.
2. Define required control before action.
3. Distinguish CoG, Schwerpunkt, decisive point, and vulnerability.
4. Concentrate where decisive; economize elsewhere.
5. Preserve material, temporal, political, and decisional reserve.
6. Treat plans as hypotheses under interaction.
7. Orient before accelerating.
8. Decentralize method without fragmenting intent.
9. Anticipate culmination before visible success.
10. Evaluate local victory by its effect on superior purpose and sustaining cooperation.
11. Separate facts, hypotheses, attributed intentions, and unknowns.
12. Verify in proportion to irreversibility.
13. Preserve options; commit only when credibility gains justify rigidity.
14. Turn recurrent conflict into minimum institutional architecture.
15. Connect pressure and compliance through comprehensible conditions and consequences.
16. Design termination and conservation from the beginning.

## 19. AI and autonomy: quarantine rule

The doctrine above is stable across human and machine execution. AI does not receive a separate strategic logic merely because it acts faster.

Prospective claims about saturation, compressed authorization, autonomous escalation, or machine-mediated termination remain **structured conjectures** unless case-specific evidence supports their use.

Operationally safe implications that follow from the non-prospective doctrine are:

- clearer purpose as action speed rises;
- explicit decision rights;
- proportional verification;
- bounded authority;
- reserves and reversible options;
- independent evidence;
- explicit stop authority;
- preserved ability to transfer and terminate.

Do not cite the existence of a speculative AI pattern as proof that it is occurring in the current case.

## 21. Final rule

The framework exists to improve judgment, not to create strategy theatre.

If a field, metric, framework, agent, meeting, dashboard, or procedure cannot change a plausible decision, its burden of proof rises. Tools remain subordinate to the result they were created to serve.
